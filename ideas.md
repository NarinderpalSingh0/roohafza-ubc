# Chosen Direction: Kinetic Refreshment

## Design Movement

**Contemporary editorial packaging meets 1970s soft-drink poster art.** The site uses the energy of outdoor food-and-beverage advertising—bold field color, oversized type, graphic curves, and distinct campaign cards—without imitating another brand’s copy, logo, packaging, or imagery.

## Core Principles

1. **One big taste moment per section:** Every panel has one clear visual event: the crimson bottle, a flavor card, a gleaming cup, or a material message.
2. **Curves disrupt the grid:** Long sinuous ribbons, ellipses, and soft-edged frames keep the page feeling fluid rather than boxed-in.
3. **Bright workhorse contrast:** Cream, vermilion, paprika, ink, and citrus create immediate legibility before decoration.
4. **Designed restraint:** Decorative bubbles and checker textures frame the hierarchy; they never cover important text or product silhouettes.

## Color Philosophy

Crimson is the emotional pulse—confident, warm, and appetite-forward. It is balanced by **vanilla cream** for breathing room, **inky cola brown** for editorial anchoring, and two precise flavor notes: **tangerine orange** and **electric lime**. The palette has the warmth of a printed soda sign rather than a glossy cyber gradient.

## Layout Paradigm

The page is a **sequence of campaign placards**, not a centered corporate grid. A full-width red masthead leads into a collage of offset cards, a wavy horizontal flavor strip, a large split editorial panel, and an asymmetric finale. Content is aligned to deliberately shifting baselines, with generous voids where the product imagery can breathe.

## Signature Elements

1. **Spark ribbon:** A looping cream line that weaves around the hero bottle and reappears as a section marker.
2. **Dot-disc sunbursts:** Halftone-style circular fields that add depth and campaign rhythm without invoking the source brand.
3. **Taste tabs:** Small, uppercase labeled pills carrying flavor or category metadata—more like packaging annotations than conventional badges.

## Interaction Philosophy

Interactions should feel like a cold can responding to a hand: crisp, light, and a little buoyant. Primary buttons compress slightly on press, taste cards lift by a few pixels, and the hero product shifts gently inside its frame on precise-pointer devices. No interaction blocks access to content.

## Animation

The hero enters in three deliberate beats: red field first, label card second, bottle and fizz last. Subsequent sections use short opacity-and-transform reveals with 50–80ms staggering. The product frame enables a capped pointer parallax of no more than 8px. All decorative motion is disabled for reduced-motion preferences; touch devices retain a composed static scene.

## Typography System

**DM Serif Display** carries the high-contrast, celebratory display statements in large sentence case. **Manrope** supplies compact, highly legible UI labels and body copy. Headlines are generous, uncompressed, and break on expressive phrases; metadata uses small uppercase Manrope with open tracking. No generic sans-only hierarchy.

## Brand Essence

**Crimson Spark is a bold sparkling-drink ritual for people who want their pause to feel like a small event.**

**Personality:** spirited, polished, warm.

## Brand Voice

Headlines are sensory, direct, and joyfully concise. CTAs use invitations and verbs rather than generic conversion language. Microcopy names a feeling or a flavor fact.

> “Make the break a little brighter.”

> “Catch the first sparkle.”

## Wordmark & Logo

The wordmark is an expressive, slightly flared serif set in cream and kept apart from navigation. The logo mark is an abstract **three-lobed spark drop**: a centered droplet pinched by two curved, radiating strokes, suggesting fizz rising from a bottle. It is rendered as a bold cream symbol on transparent background and remains clear at favicon scale.

## Signature Brand Color

**Spark Crimson — #E61728.** A high-energy but warm red that belongs to the brand’s product chamber, controls, and most recognisable visual moments.
